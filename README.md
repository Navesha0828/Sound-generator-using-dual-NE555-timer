# Sound Generator Using Dual NE555 Timer ICs

## Overview
An electronic sound generator built using two NE555 timer ICs configured in astable and monostable modes. An LM386 audio amplifier is used to amplify the generated signal and drive a speaker, while a potentiometer provides volume control.

## Objectives
- To design and construct an electronic sound-generation circuit using NE555 timer ICs.
- To understand the operation of astable and monostable multivibrator configurations.
- To study how resistor-capacitor (RC) networks influence timing and signal generation.
- To explore the practical application of digital ICs and audio amplification.

## Components Used
- 2 × NE555 Timer ICs
- LM386 Audio Amplifier IC
- Resistors
- Capacitors
- 10 kΩ Potentiometer
- Six switches
- Speaker
- 9 V DC Power Supply
- Breadboard and connecting wires

## Working Principle
The circuit uses two NE555 timer ICs to generate sound signals.

1. **First NE555 – Astable Mode:** Generates a continuous pulse signal. The resistor-switch network changes the timing resistance, producing variations in the generated signal.
2. **Second NE555 – Monostable Mode:** Produces timed output pulses in response to trigger signals from the first timer.
3. **LM386 Audio Amplifier:** Amplifies the generated signal so that it can drive a speaker.
4. **Potentiometer:** Adjusts the volume of the audio output.

The combination of timing circuits and audio amplification produces different electronic sound patterns.
